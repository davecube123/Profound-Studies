Experiment: does the pivot rule matter?

Counts comparisons instantly (no animation) for each input type and pivot rule. Random-pivot results are averaged over 30 runs. Guess the answers before you press the button.

What to notice

  • With first-element pivot on sorted input, the call stack becomes a staircase n levels deep. Every split peels off just one element.
  • With a random pivot, the stack stays about 2·log₂ n deep on every input. The bad luck has moved from the data to the coin, and the coin is fair.
  • Proof idea for the average: items of rank i and j are compared only if one of them is the first pivot picked from ranks i…j, which has probability 2/(j−i+1). Summing over all pairs gives ≈ 2n ln n.
