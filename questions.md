## Seed questions (starting points)

- Is there one precise mathematical sense in which "simple rules create complexity"? (Kolmogorov complexity? Computational irreducibility?)
- Why does the *random* model of primes predict twin primes so well, when primes are not random at all?
- Quicksort with a random pivot is fast on *every* input on average. What exactly does "on average" mean there, and how is that different from "on average over inputs"?
- Gödel's sentence and a quine both refer to themselves. Is it the *same* trick? (Hint: the diagonal lemma.)
- If the Game of Life can compute anything, can a Life pattern "prove" Gödel's theorem about itself?
- Does randomness truly add power to computation, or is it always an illusion (P = BPP)?
- What would it *feel* like to live in a universe where P = NP?
